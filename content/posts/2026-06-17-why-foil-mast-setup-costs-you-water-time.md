## What Screw-Based Foil Mast Mounting Actually Costs

The bolt-and-baseplate system was inherited from windsurfing. In that era, you rigged once, sailed for hours, de-rigged once. A five-minute setup was irrelevant against a six-hour session.

Foil riding broke that math. Modern foil riders go out three, four, five times a week. They swap boards. They travel. They launch from spots where there's no flat ground to work on and no table to set tools down.

Here's what the screw-based foil mast setup costs per session:

**Hardware attrition.** M6 bolts don't survive indefinitely in saltwater and sand. Threads corrode. Bolts strip. The baseplate walks. A single missed bolt or undertorqued connection is a failure point mid-session — not just slow, but a genuine safety liability.

**Setup variance.** Manual torquing is inconsistent. The mast position after bolt three is not identical to the position after bolt four. For pump foilers calibrating their technique to millimeter-level positional accuracy, that variance accumulates into real performance loss.

**The 10-minute clock.** Even a fast, practiced install on a traditional foil mast mounting system takes three to seven minutes. Add one complication — cold fingers, a sandy track, a stripped hex socket — and you're at ten. Per session. Every session.

The real cost isn't time. It's the mental overhead: are all four bolts seated correctly? Did I torque evenly? Is there movement in the plate? You're thinking about hardware when you should be thinking about water.
