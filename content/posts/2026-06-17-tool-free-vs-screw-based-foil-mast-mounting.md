## The Problem: Screws, Lost Parts, and Time You're Not Riding

Every foil rider knows this moment. The conditions align. The forecast was right. You drove to the spot, rigged the wing, pumped the board, and then — you're kneeling in the sand with a hex key, chasing four M6 bolts through the standard foil mast mounting system.

The screw-based foil mast connection is a legacy design. It was adapted from windsurfing hardware built for once-a-week sessions where a two-minute setup penalty doesn't register. Apply that same system to modern foil riding — riders going out four times a week, switching between boards, traveling with gear — and the friction compounds fast.

Here's what "screw-based" actually costs you:

**Lost hardware.** M6 bolts are small. Sandy beaches and dark water don't return them. Experienced foilers carry spare kits because losing a single bolt means an aborted session. That's not a contingency; that's an admission that the system has a known failure mode.

**Setup time.** A clean, practiced install on a traditional foil mast mounting system takes three to five minutes minimum — torquing four bolts evenly, checking for plate movement, re-torquing after the first immersion when hardware seats. Multiply that by the number of sessions in a season and you're looking at hours spent on assembly, not riding.

**Thread degradation.** Saltwater and repeated torque cycles erode aluminum threads. By the second season, most riders are already replacing baseplates or installing thread inserts. A mounting system shouldn't be a maintenance item.

**Position drift.** Manual torquing introduces subtle variance. The mast position after bolt three isn't identical to the position after bolt four. For wingfoilers, this is tolerable. For pump foilers whose entire technique is calibrated to a specific mast position, it's a genuine performance liability.

The screw-based foil mast mounting system wasn't designed badly — it was designed for a different era of the sport. MastLOCK was designed for this one.
