## The Problem With Screws

Screw-based mast mounting has three structural failure modes that compound over a season.

**Torque variance.** A hand-tightened bolt delivers wildly inconsistent clamping force. Under-tighten and the mast shifts under load — you feel it as vague, unpredictable feedback on the foil. Over-tighten and you're one hard landing away from a stripped thread or a cracked baseplate. There's no tactile window between the two.

**Saltwater corrosion.** Titanium, stainless, aluminium — they all corrode at the galvanic junctions where dissimilar metals meet saltwater. After forty sessions, what started as a snug M6 thread is now a 20-minute extraction project. The screw doesn't fail dramatically; it just gets progressively worse until a session day becomes a repair day.

**Repeat-setup degradation.** Every removal cycle abrades the thread interface. Modern carbon foil masts are stiff and precise — the mounting interface shouldn't introduce the variability the mast was engineered to eliminate. But screw systems accumulate microdamage exactly where tolerances matter most.

The result: a mounting mechanism that degrades every single time you use it. That's not acceptable engineering.
